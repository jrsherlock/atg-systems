Internet-exposed automatic tank gauges across the United States

A nationwide census of Veeder-Root ATG controllers reachable on the public internet, enriched with Shodan geolocation, network ownership, and banner-exposure analysis.

Snapshot: June 3, 2026
Source: Shodan census
Fingerprint: port:10001 "I20100"
On June 2, 2026, CISA, the FBI, EPA, and DOE issued the joint fact sheet Hardening Automatic Tank Gauge Systems after active compromise of internet-exposed ATGs. These controllers run live fuel sites. This census counts what is exposed today and, where the device leaks it, what each controller reveals: the site's name, street address, and live tank levels. The fingerprint port:10001 "I20100" identifies Veeder-Root controllers; honeypots and cloud sensors are separated from likely field devices using Shodan's own classification tags.

Method and sources

Census. Shodan, June 3, 2026, fingerprint port:10001 "I20100" country:US. Port 10001 is the Veeder-Root TLS service; I20100 is the in-tank inventory command. Global population: 5,612. U.S.: 2,326 hosts.

Classification. Likely field devices (1,860, 80%) exclude cloud and hosting networks. Shodan tags 230 hosts as honeypots and 1,313 as ICS devices. Leak rates are computed only over the 1,768 non-honeypot field devices, so decoy banners do not inflate them.

Exposure. Each controller's banner is parsed for a configured site header (name, street, city/state/ZIP) and a live in-tank inventory. Only boolean leak flags, tank counts, and generic product types are retained. No site name, street address, ZIP, or full IP appears anywhere in this file.

No vulnerability data. Shodan records no CVEs for this device class (the service exposes a serial protocol, not a fingerprintable stack). Known Veeder-Root advisories live in CISA's ICS catalog. CISA · Shodan.

Prepared by ProCircular. A point-in-time snapshot; counts drift as devices appear, move, or go offline.
